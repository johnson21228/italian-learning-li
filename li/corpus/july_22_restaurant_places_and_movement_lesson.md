## What the lesson appears to be doing

The lesson moves through a practical sequence:

```text
say what you have or need
→ order food and drink
→ identify restaurant objects and meal courses
→ name places
→ say where you go
→ say who comes
→ use a taxi to reach a destination
```

This fits the Workbench's conversation-first posture. The verbs matter because
they help the learner act in a real situation.

## 1. Avere — to have

The class reviews the present tense of `avere`.

| Person | Italian | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| io | ho | I have |
| tu | hai | you have |
| lui / lei / Lei | ha | he / she / you formal have |
| noi | abbiamo | we have |
| voi | avete | you all have |
| loro | hanno | they have |

Two useful expressions shown with `avere` are:

```text
Ho fame. — I am hungry. Literally: I have hunger.
Ho sete. — I am thirsty. Literally: I have thirst.
```

The taxi material later reuses the same verb:

```text
Ho bisogno di un taxi. — I need a taxi.
```

The ordering prompt also distinguishes:

### What are you ordering?

The visible classroom prompt is:

```text
Cosa ordini?
Cosa ordini da mangiare e da bere?
```

Meaning:

```text
What are you ordering?
What are you ordering to eat and drink?
```

The class asks for a complete response beginning with:

```text
Vorrei... — I would like...
Desidero... — I would like... / I desire...
```

Useful reconstructed answers:

```text

Desidero un bicchiere d'acqua.
I would like a glass of water.

Per cena vorrei un secondo con un contorno.
For dinner I would like a second course with a side dish.
```

### Useful restaurant expressions

The screenshots show:

```text
Vorrei... — I would like...
Il conto, per piacere. — The check, please.
Desidero... — I would like... / I desire...
Il menù, per favore. — The menu, please.
Si accetta...? — Do you accept...?
Mi piace... — I like... [one thing or an infinitive]
Mi piacciono... — I like... [plural things]
senza... — without...
con... — with...
Servizio incluso — Service included
Il coperto — Cover charge
```

Small speaking exchange:

```text

Cameriera: Desidera altro?
Steve: No, grazie.
```

## 5. Venire — to come

### Provenance

The topic slide says:

```text
Andare e venire:
I luoghi ed il trasporto pubblico
```

The learner directly recalls that both “to go” and “to come” were covered.
The full table below is therefore a careful reconstruction, not a transcription
of a visible conjugation slide.

| Person | Italian | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| io | vengo | I come / I am coming |
| tu | vieni | you come / you are coming |
| lui / lei / Lei | viene | he / she / you formal comes |
| noi | veniamo | we come / we are coming |
| voi | venite | you all come / are coming |
| loro | vengono | they come / are coming |

Conversation:

```text
Vieni al ristorante?
Sì, vengo.

Venite con noi?
Sì, veniamo con voi.

Chi viene al cinema?
Viene Pamela.
```

### Go versus come

Use `andare` when movement is away from the speaker's current point of view:

```text
Vado al mercato. — I am going to the market.
```

Use `venire` when movement is toward the speaker, listener, or shared
destination under discussion:

```text
Vieni con noi? — Are you coming with us?
```

## 6. Taxi and public transportation

The screenshot gives a compact taxi exchange:

```text
Dove sono i taxi? — Where are the taxis?
Ho bisogno di un taxi. — I need a taxi.
A... — To...
Alla stazione, per favore. — To the station, please.
All'aeroporto, per favore. — To the airport, please.
Al centro, per favore. — Downtown, please.
All'hotel... — To the hotel...
A questo indirizzo, per favore. — To this address, please.
Quanto le devo? — How much do I owe you?
```

A complete practice exchange:

```text
Steve: Ho bisogno di un taxi.
Autista: Dove va?
Steve: Alla stazione, per favore.
Autista: Va bene.
Steve: Quanto le devo?
```
